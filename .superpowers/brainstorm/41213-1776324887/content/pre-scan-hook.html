What do they see before entering their URL?

You have 3 seconds to convince them to type. What's the hook?

A

"How much money is your website losing you?"

Direct. Painful. The input field sits right below with placeholder text: yourbusiness.com and a button: "Scan Now — Free". Nothing else. No sub-copy. Just the question and the action.

B

"Your website has problems. Let's find them."

Confrontational but helpful. One line of sub-copy: "Free 30-second scan. No email required." Input with placeholder: Enter your website and button: "Run Diagnostic"

C

"We scanned 200+ local businesses this month. Yours is next."

Social proof + curiosity. Sub-copy: "Takes 30 seconds. Shows you exactly what's costing you customers." Input with placeholder: yourbusiness.com and button: "Scan My Business"

D

Terminal-first. No headline.

The whole hero IS the terminal. Full screen dark with a blinking cursor: $ brewington --scan ___________. Underneath in small mono text: "Paste your website. See what's broken. Free." The aesthetic does the selling.

IMPORTANT

Remember: visitors who skip or don't enter a URL need a fallback. After ~8 seconds of no input, the default scan auto-plays so they still see the show. We also need a small "Skip" link somewhere.
