# Google Business Profile Strategy for Brewington Digital

## Goal
Set up and optimize a Google Business Profile for Brewington Digital so local service business owners searching for website help or marketing help find Lando organically. Then use GBP as an ongoing content and visibility channel.

---

## Step 1: Create the Profile

### Business Information
- **Business Name:** Brewington Digital
- **Primary Category:** Internet Marketing Service
- **Secondary Categories:** Web Designer, Website Designer, Marketing Agency, Business to Business Service
- **Phone:** Your main business number (the one connected to GHL)
- **Website:** brewingtondigital.carrd.co (update when you have a full site)
- **Address/Service Area:** See the service area section below

### Service Area vs Storefront
Set up as a **Service Area Business (SAB)**, not a storefront. You do not have a physical location clients visit. SAB lets you define your service area without showing a street address.

### Handling the Nationwide Service Area
This is the tricky part. Google lets you define service areas by city, county, state, or country. Here is the play:

**Option A (Recommended for now):** Set your service area to the United States. This is broad but honest. You serve businesses nationwide. Google will primarily show you to searchers in your local area (Utah) but the national designation keeps you eligible for broader searches.

**Option B (Targeted approach):** Set specific metro areas where you are actively prospecting. Start with:
- Salt Lake City, UT (home base)
- Mesa, AZ / Scottsdale, AZ / Gilbert, AZ (where your current prospects are)
- Add more cities as you expand

Option B is better for ranking in specific markets. You can change this anytime.

## Step 4: Get the First 5 Reviews (With Zero Clients)

You need reviews to rank and to build trust. Here is how to get them before you have paying clients:

### Review Source 1: People You Have Helped for Free
Anyone you have given website advice to, built a demo for, or helped with their online presence. Even if they did not become a client. Text them:

"Hey [name], I know we talked about your business's online presence a while back. Would you be willing to leave a quick Google review for Brewington Digital? Even just a sentence about the advice or help I gave you. Would mean a lot as I am getting this thing off the ground."

### Review Source 2: Brian and Close Friends
People who know your work ethic and what you are building. They can speak to your character and dedication even if they are not clients.

"Hey, I just set up my Google Business Profile for Brewington Digital. Would you mind leaving me a review? You can talk about anything: my work ethic, what you have seen me build, whatever feels honest. Here is the link: [direct review link]"

### Review Source 3: GHL Community and Online Connections
People you have helped in Facebook groups, Reddit, or GHL communities. If you gave them useful advice, ask:

"Hey, you mentioned my advice was helpful. Would you be willing to leave a quick review on Google? Trying to get my first few reviews up. Here is the link."

### Review Source 4: Your First Demo Recipients
Even if they do not become clients, if they liked the demo site, ask for a review about the experience.

### How to Get the Direct Review Link
1. Search for your business on Google
2. Click "Write a review" on your own listing
3. Copy that URL
4. Use a URL shortener to make it easy to text

### Target: 5 reviews in the first 2 weeks. 10 reviews by end of month 1.

## Step 6: GBP Posts as a Content Channel

Google Business Profile posts show up on your listing and can influence rankings. Post 2x per week minimum.

"Just finished building a new website for an HVAC company in Arizona. Mobile-friendly, click-to-call button at the top, online booking built in. This is what every service business website should look like in 2026."

**Type 3: Before and After**
"Before: A 20-year electrician with zero online presence. After: Professional website, 15 new Google reviews in 60 days, and a missed-call text-back system that saves 10+ leads per month. This is what a complete online system looks like."

**Type 4: Offers**

### Post Format

- Include a photo with every post
- Add a call-to-action button ("Learn More," "Call Now," or "Book Online")
- Use relevant keywords naturally (do not stuff them)

## Step 7: How Google Ranks GBP Listings

Three main factors:

### 1. Relevance
How well your profile matches what someone searched for. This is why your categories, description, and posts matter. If someone searches "marketing for plumbers," your description should contain those concepts.

### 2. Distance
How close you are to the searcher. For a service-area business, this is based on your service area settings. This is why targeting specific metros matters more than just "United States."

### 3. Prominence
How well-known your business is online. This includes:
- Number and quality of reviews
- How often you post
- How many photos you have
- Whether your business is mentioned on other websites
- How complete your profile is

### What Moves the Needle Most
1. **Reviews** - More reviews with higher ratings = higher ranking. Responding to reviews also helps.
2. **Profile completeness** - Fill out every single field Google offers. Hours, services, description, photos, Q&A.
3. **Posting frequency** - Active profiles rank better than dormant ones.
4. **Website link** - Having a real website (not just Carrd) will help long-term.
5. **Consistent NAP** - Your Name, Address, Phone number should be identical everywhere online.

## Step 8: Common GBP Mistakes to Avoid

1. **Stuffing keywords in your business name.** Your name is "Brewington Digital." Not "Brewington Digital - Best Website Design and Marketing for HVAC Plumbing Electrical." Google will suspend your listing.
2. **Setting up as a storefront when you are a SAB.** You do not need a physical address visible. Service area is the right setup.
3. **Ignoring reviews.** Every review gets a response. Good or bad. Within 24 hours.
4. **Posting once and forgetting.** GBP rewards consistency. 2 posts per week minimum.
5. **Using stock photos.** Real screenshots, real work, real results. Stock photos make you look fake.
6. **Not tracking insights.** Check monthly to see what searches lead people to your profile. Adjust your description and posts based on what people actually search for.
7. **Having different info across platforms.** If your phone number on GBP does not match your website and your Carrd page, Google gets confused.
8. **Not using the Q&A section.** Seed your own Q&A. Ask questions you know clients would ask, then answer them yourself. Google shows these on your listing.
