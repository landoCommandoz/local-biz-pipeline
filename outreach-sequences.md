# Cold Text and Email Outreach Sequences

## The Rules
Every message sounds like a real person wrote it. Someone who actually looked at their business and noticed a specific problem. No corporate language. No filler. No "I hope this finds you well." The goal of every message is ONE thing: get them to reply. Not close them. Not pitch them. Just start a conversation.

---

## Cold Text Sequence

### Text 1: The Opener (Day 1)

**Template:**
"Hey [First Name], this is Lando. I was looking at [trade] companies in [city] and came across [Business Name]. Noticed [specific problem]. Not trying to sell you anything, just thought you should know. That one thing is probably costing you [specific consequence]."

**Example 1 (Sunstar Heating & Aire):**
"Hey, this is Lando. I was looking at HVAC companies in Mesa and came across Sunstar Heating. I noticed you do not have a website at all, just directory listings. That means every time someone Googles 'HVAC Mesa' you are invisible unless they happen to scroll through Yelp. Just thought you should know."

**Example 2 (Lightech Electric Co):**
"Hey, this is Lando. I was looking at electricians in Scottsdale and found Lightech Electric. You have been in business over 20 years but I could not find a website for you anywhere. That means every new customer searching online right now is going straight to your competitors. Figured you would want to know."

**Example 3 (Young Plumbing LLC):**
"Hey, this is Lando. I was looking at plumbing companies in Gilbert and found Young Plumbing. You have a perfect 5.0 rating which is great, but you only have a handful of reviews. That is the difference between showing up on Google and getting buried. One thing could fix that pretty fast."

### Text 2: The Follow-Up (Day 3)

**Template:**
"Hey [First Name], sent you a text a couple days ago about [their specific problem]. Not trying to bug you. Just see this a lot with [trade] companies and it is usually a quick fix. Worth a conversation if you have 5 minutes."

**Example 1 (PL Miller Electric):**
"Hey, sent you a text a couple days ago about PL Miller's online presence. For a company that has been around 50 years, you should be dominating online. You are not even close right now. Worth a quick conversation if you have a few minutes."

**Example 2 (Evolution Plumbing):**
"Hey, followed up on my text about Evolution Plumbing. You have two separate websites competing against each other right now, evolutionplumbingaz.com and evolutionplumbinggilbert.com. That is actually hurting you on Google. Quick fix if you are interested."

**Example 3 (Pink Plumbing & Sewer):**
"Hey, followed up on my text about Pink Plumbing. I tried to call from your website and could not even find the phone number easily. If I had trouble finding it, your customers definitely are too. That is money walking out the door."

### Text 3: The Value Drop (Day 8)

**Template:**
"Hey [First Name], last follow-up from me. I actually put together a quick breakdown of what [Business Name] looks like online right now vs what your top competitors look like. If you want to see it, just say the word and I will send it over. If not, no worries. I know you are busy."

**Example 1 (Indoor Comfort Experts):**
"Hey, last message from me. I actually looked at how Indoor Comfort Experts shows up compared to the top 3 HVAC companies in Mesa. Your domain iceiceac.com does not match your business name, your ratings are low, and your competitors are pulling the customers you should be getting. I put together a quick breakdown if you want to see it. If not, no worries."

**Example 2 (Independent Electric Co):**
"Hey, last follow-up. I looked at Independent Electric compared to other electricians in Scottsdale. You have been in business since 1996 but only have 4 reviews online. Your competitors with half your experience have 50-100+. I put together a breakdown of what is happening and how to fix it. Want me to send it over?"

**Example 3 (Scottsdale Plumbing / Handy Dan's):**
"Hey, last message. I noticed Scottsdale Plumbing Service and Handy Dan's are the same business but they show up as two different companies online. That confuses Google and confuses customers. I mapped out what is going on if you want to see it. Just say the word."

---

### Text 4: The Breakup (Day 15)

**Template:**
"Hey [First Name], I will leave you alone after this. If you ever want a second set of eyes on how [Business Name] shows up online, my number is right here. Good luck with everything."

Keep this one short. No pressure. No guilt. Just a clean exit that leaves the door open.

## Cold Email Sequence

### Email 1: The Observation (Day 1)

**Subject Line Options:**
- "Quick question about [Business Name]"
- "[Business Name] on Google"
- "Noticed something about [Business Name]"
- "Your competitors in [city] are doing this"

**Template:**
Subject: Quick question about [Business Name]

[First Name],

I was researching [trade] companies in [city] and came across [Business Name]. 

I noticed [specific problem, 1-2 sentences]. That is [specific consequence for their business].

I work with [trade] companies on their online presence and see this exact problem all the time. It is usually fixable without a huge investment of time or money.

Would it be worth a quick conversation? I can text or email, whichever is easier for you.

Lando Brewington
Brewington Digital

**Example 1 (Mesa HVAC Contractor):**

All of that is fixable. I work with HVAC companies on this stuff every day.

Would it be worth a quick text conversation? No pitch, just want to show you what I see.

Lando Brewington
Brewington Digital

## Best Times to Send

### Texts
- **Best:** Tuesday through Thursday, 7:00-8:00 AM local time (before they get on jobs)
- **Second best:** Tuesday through Thursday, 5:00-7:00 PM local time (after jobs, before dinner)
- **Avoid:** Monday mornings (overwhelmed), Friday afternoons (checked out), weekends

### Emails
- **Best:** Tuesday through Thursday, 6:00-7:00 AM local time (top of inbox when they check)
- **Second best:** Tuesday through Wednesday, 8:00-9:00 PM local time (evening inbox check)
- **Avoid:** Same as texts

## Response Playbook

### When They Reply Positively
"Sounds interesting" / "Tell me more" / "What do you have in mind?"

**Response:** "Appreciate you getting back to me. The short version: I build a complete online system for [trade] companies. Professional website, automated text-back when you miss a call, review requests that go out after every job, and a booking page so customers can schedule without calling. Everything runs automatically. $297-$497/month depending on what you need. Want me to show you what it would look like for [Business Name]? I can put together a quick preview."

### When They Reply Negatively
"Not interested" / "Stop texting me" / "I'm good"

**Response:** "Totally understand. If anything changes down the road, my number is right here. Good luck with everything."

Never argue. Never follow up again unless they reach out first.

### When They Ask "How Much?"

**Response:** "Depends on what you need, but most [trade] companies I work with are between $297 and $497 a month. That covers the website, automated missed-call text-back, review requests, appointment booking, and monthly reporting. Everything runs without you having to think about it. Want me to break down which pieces would make the biggest difference for [Business Name] specifically?"

### When They Ask "What Exactly Do You Do?"

**Response:** "I build done-for-you online systems for service businesses. Here is what that looks like: a professional website built around your business, a system that automatically texts back customers when you miss a call, automated review requests after every completed job, online booking so customers can schedule without playing phone tag, and a monthly report showing what is working. Everything is set up for you, runs automatically, and costs between $297-$497/month. The goal is simple: you do the work, the system makes sure you never lose a customer because of your online presence."

### When They Say "Let Me Think About It"

**Response:** "No rush at all. If it helps, I can put together a quick preview of what [Business Name] would look like with the full system. That way you have something concrete to think about instead of just an idea. Want me to do that?"

### When They Ghost (No Response at All)

Continue the text/email sequence as written. After the breakup message, add them to a 90-day follow-up list. One text in 90 days: "Hey [First Name], this is Lando. Reached out a while back about [Business Name]. Just checking in. Anything changed?"

## Two-Week Outreach Cadence

**Week 1:**
- Monday: Send Text 1 to prospects 1-3
- Tuesday: Send Email 1 to prospects 1-3
- Wednesday: Send Text 1 to prospects 4-6
- Thursday: Send Email 1 to prospects 4-6, Send Text 2 to prospects 1-3
- Friday: Send Email 2 to prospects 1-3, Send Text 1 to prospects 7-8

**Week 2:**
- Monday: Send Text 2 to prospects 4-6, Send Email 1 to prospects 7-8
- Tuesday: Send Text 1 to prospects 9-10, Send Email 2 to prospects 4-6
- Wednesday: Send Text 3 to prospects 1-3, Send Text 2 to prospects 7-8
- Thursday: Send Email 1 to prospects 9-10, Send Email 3 to prospects 1-3
- Friday: Send Text 3 to prospects 4-6, Send Text 2 to prospects 9-10

Continue this pattern. Stagger so you never have more than 4-5 sends per day. Track responses in a simple spreadsheet or in GHL.
