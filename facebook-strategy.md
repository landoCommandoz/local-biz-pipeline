# Facebook Groups Strategy for Brewington Digital

## Goal
Get inside the rooms where HVAC, plumbing, electrical, and landscaping business owners already talk to each other. Become a trusted voice. Convert group members into text or email leads without getting banned. No ads. No budget. Just time and consistency.

---

## Group Categories to Join

### Category 1: Trade-Specific Owner Groups
These are where business OWNERS hang out, not technicians. Look for groups with "business," "owner," or "company" in the name.

- **HVAC Business Owners** / **HVAC Contractors** - Multiple groups with 5k-30k members
- **Plumbing Business Owners** / **Plumbing Contractors Group**
- **Electrical Contractor Network** / **Electrician Business Owners**
- **Landscaping Business Owners** / **Lawn Care Millionaire** community groups
- **Pressure Washing Business Owners**
- **Cleaning Company Owners** / **Maid Service Business Owners**
- **Pest Control Business Owners**
- **Roofing Contractors Group**

**Search terms to find these:** "[trade] business owners," "[trade] contractors group," "[trade] company owners"

### Category 2: General Home Service and Contractor Groups
- **Home Service Business Owners** (multiple versions, 10k-50k members)
- **Service Business Owners Network**
- **Blue Collar Business Owners**
- **Contractor Business Growth**
- **Home Service Millionaires**
- **Service Titan Users Group** (software-specific, but full of owners)

### Category 3: Small Business Owner Groups
- **Small Business Owners Community** (various, some have 100k+ members)
- **Entrepreneur and Small Business Owners**
- **Small Business Networking**

### Category 4: Marketing and Growth Groups for Service Businesses
- **GoHighLevel Official Community** (GHL users, potential referral partners AND clients)
- **Digital Marketing for Small Business**
- **Local Business Marketing**

### Category 5: Software User Groups
- **Jobber Users Group** (scheduling software for service businesses)
- **Housecall Pro Community**
- **ServiceTitan Users**

These are goldmines. People in these groups already spend money on business tools. They understand paying monthly for something that helps their business. Easiest upsell ever.

### How Many to Join
Start with 8-10 groups. Do not join 30 at once. Facebook flags accounts that join too many groups too fast. Join 2-3 per week for the first month.

## Profile Optimization (Do This First)

Before you join a single group, your personal Facebook profile needs to look right. Group admins check profiles before approving members.

1. **Profile photo:** Professional headshot or clean photo of you. Not a logo. Not a graphic.
2. **Cover photo:** Something related to what you do. Could be a screenshot of a site you built, a simple branded graphic, or a photo of you working.
3. **Bio/Intro:** "I build websites and automated systems for service businesses. HVAC, plumbing, electrical, landscaping." Keep it simple.
4. **Featured section:** Pin a post about your work. A before/after website screenshot. A testimonial. Something that shows what you do without being an ad.
5. **Recent posts:** Make sure your last 5-10 public posts are not all sales content. Mix in personal stuff, observations about business, helpful tips.

### Join Question Answers
Most groups ask 2-3 questions when you request to join. Answer honestly:

- "What type of business do you own?" - "I run a web and automation company that works with service businesses."
- "Why do you want to join?" - "I work with HVAC/plumbing/electrical companies every day and want to connect with other people in the industry."
- "Will you follow the group rules?" - "Yes" (read them, actually follow them)

Never say "I want to offer my services" or anything that sounds like you are there to sell.

## The 30-Day Trust-Building Strategy

### Week 1: Listen and Learn (Days 1-7)
- Scroll through each group daily. Read what people post about.
- Identify the top 3-5 problems people complain about repeatedly
- Note who the active members are, who the admins are, who gets the most engagement
- Do NOT post anything yet. Just like and react to helpful posts.
- Drop 1-2 short, helpful comments per day on other people's posts

### Week 2: Start Contributing (Days 8-14)
- Comment on 3-5 posts per day with genuinely useful input
- When someone asks about websites, online presence, or getting more customers, give real advice
- Share a tip you learned from working with service businesses (no mention of your company)
- Start conversations in comments. Ask follow-up questions.

### Week 3: Establish Presence (Days 15-21)
- Make your first original post in 2-3 groups. Something helpful, not promotional.
- Continue commenting daily
- By now, people should start recognizing your name
- When someone thanks you for advice, that is your opening for a DM

### Week 4: Start Converting (Days 22-30)
- You have built enough goodwill to start having private conversations
- When you see someone struggling with something you solve, comment with value, then DM with an offer to help
- Ask for their number or email to continue the conversation off Facebook

## Comment Templates

### When Someone Asks "How Do I Get More Customers?"
"Biggest thing I see with [trade] companies is they lose half their leads before they even talk to them. Someone calls, nobody answers, they call the next guy on Google. If you set up an automated text that fires back within 30 seconds of a missed call saying 'Hey, sorry I missed you. I am on a job right now. What do you need help with?' you will save at least 5-10 leads a month. That alone can be worth $2-5k depending on your average ticket."

### When Someone Complains About Bad Reviews
"Here is what works: after every job, before you even leave the driveway, send the customer a text with a direct link to your Google review page. Not a generic 'leave us a review' email three days later. A text. Right then. While they are still happy. Do that consistently for 90 days and you will bury those bad reviews with good ones."

### When Someone Posts About Their Website
"Honest question, can you pull up your website on your phone right now and find your phone number without scrolling? If not, that is the first thing to fix. 70%+ of people looking for a [plumber/electrician/HVAC company] are on their phone. If they cannot tap to call in the first two seconds, they bounce."

### When Someone Asks About Marketing Agencies
"I will be real with you. Most agencies do not understand service businesses. They will charge you $1,500/month to run Facebook ads to people who are not looking for a plumber right now. What actually moves the needle: Google Business Profile, reviews, answering your phone or having a system that does it for you, and a website that makes it easy to call or book. That is it. Everything else is a bonus."

"Quick tip for anyone running a [trade] company: check your Google Business Profile right now. If your hours are wrong, your phone number is wrong, or you have not posted anything in the last 30 days, you are invisible to half the people searching for you. Takes 10 minutes to fix. Free. And it matters more than almost anything else you could spend time on this week."

But he looked up his business on Google and his listing still had his old phone number from three years ago. He had no idea. Meanwhile his competitor down the street with half the experience was getting all the Google calls because his profile was actually up to date. This stuff matters more than most people realize."

### Engagement Post: The Question
"For the [trade] owners in here, what is the one thing you wish you had set up from day one that would have saved you the most time? For me, working with service companies, the answer is almost always automated follow-up. But I am curious what you all would say."

## The Bridge: Group Comment to Client Conversation

### Method 1: The Helpful DM
After giving value in a comment, DM them:
"Hey [name], saw your post about [their problem]. I actually work with [trade] companies on exactly that. Did not want to turn your post into a sales pitch but happy to take a look at your setup if you want. What is the best way to reach you, text or email?"

### Method 2: The Resource Share
"Hey [name], I put together a quick checklist for [trade] companies to audit their online presence. Want me to send it over? What is your email?"
(You should actually have this checklist. One page. Valuable. Not a pitch.)

### Method 3: The Direct Offer
Only use this after multiple interactions with the same person:
"Hey [name], based on everything you have been posting about, I think I could actually help you fix [specific problem]. I do this for [trade] companies every day. No pressure, but if you want to see what it would look like for your business, shoot me your email and I will send over some details."

### Method 4: The Screenshot
Build a quick mockup or screenshot of what their online presence COULD look like. DM it to them:
"Hey [name], I was messing around and mocked up what your Google results could look like with a few tweaks. Thought you might find it interesting. [screenshot] Want me to walk you through what I changed?"

## What NOT to Do (How to Get Banned)

1. **Never post a link to your website in a group.** Instant ban in most groups.
2. **Never comment with "DM me" or "I can help with this, check out my..."** That is the fastest way to get flagged.
3. **Never pitch in your first week in a group.** Admins watch new members closely.
4. **Never copy-paste the same comment across multiple groups.** Facebook flags this as spam.
5. **Never argue with admins.** If they delete your post, accept it and adjust.
6. **Never post promotional content, even disguised as a question.** "Has anyone tried using a missed-call text-back system? I just set one up for my client and..." This is obvious and people hate it.
7. **Never add group members as friends without interacting first.** Comes across as harvesting.
8. **Never post more than once per week in any single group.** Even helpful posts get flagged if you post too often.

## Weekly Routine (30-45 Minutes Per Day)

### Daily (20-30 min)
1. Open your 8-10 groups
2. Scroll through new posts (5 min)
3. Comment on 3-5 posts with genuine value (15-20 min)
4. Check and respond to any DMs (5 min)

### Monday: Audit and Plan
- Check which groups had the most engagement last week
- Identify 2 groups to post original content in this week
- Note any warm conversations to follow up on

### Wednesday: Original Post Day
- Post one piece of value content in your highest-engagement group
- Continue daily commenting in other groups

### Friday: DM and Follow-Up Day
- Send DMs to anyone who engaged heavily with your comments this week
- Follow up on any open DM conversations
- Move warm leads to text or email

### Sunday: Content Prep
- Write your two posts for the upcoming week
- Prepare any screenshots or resources you want to share

## Scaling Without It Becoming Full-Time

### Tier 1 (Month 1): 8-10 groups, 30-45 min/day
Focus on getting known. Build your rhythm. Find which groups have the most active owners.

### Tier 2 (Month 2-3): Keep the top 5-6 groups, drop the dead ones
By now you know which groups actually have owners who engage. Double down there. Drop groups where nobody responds or where admins are too strict.

### Tier 3 (Month 3+): Systemize what works
- Template your best-performing comments (personalize each time, but start from a proven framework)
- Block specific times for Facebook Groups. Do not let it bleed into all-day scrolling.
- Track which groups produce actual leads. Focus there.
- Consider creating your own group once you have 5+ clients. "Local Service Business Growth" or similar. This becomes your owned audience.

---

## Expected Results

- **Week 1-2:** Zero leads. You are invisible. This is normal.
- **Week 3-4:** First DM conversations. People start recognizing your name.
- **Month 2:** 3-5 warm conversations per week from group activity.
- **Month 3:** 1-2 qualified leads per week. These are people who already trust you because they have seen you help others for free.

Facebook Groups are the fastest free channel for reaching service business owners because that is where they already spend time. The key is patience in the first two weeks and consistency after that.
